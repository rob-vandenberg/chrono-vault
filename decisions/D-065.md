# D-065: Audit

Approved by the owner on 25 November 2026 (Proceed, autonomous mode, on the changelist of version 0.6.86).
`REQUIREMENTS.md` 6.3, `ROADMAP.md` step 12.

## What was decided

1. **Five lists** of the open vault's passwords, without the Trash, the Archive and excluded items: **Leaked**,
   **Reused** (in more than one item), **Weak** (under 50 bits, by an estimate of our own: no library, no word list of
   others), **Old** (2 years unchanged; an unknown date is not old), **Breached websites** (the item's website lost
   passwords, by Have I Been Pwned's verified list, and the password has not changed since; an unknown date counts as
   not changed). The list of breaches is named as its licence asks: Have I Been Pwned, with a link.
2. **One tick box** in Settings > Security, off at first, for Leaked and Breached websites. Of a password only the first
   5 digits of its SHA-1 hash are sent, with padded answers; the breaches are fetched whole. The answers live only in
   memory, while the vault is unlocked. Not reached reads "Not checked", never safe, with "Check now".
3. **Where it is seen**: on the right with no item focused a score (the part of the passwords in none of the lists that
   are checked) and a card per list, red above 0 and green at 0; a sidebar group Audit (View > Audit); Reused with the
   items of one password together; remarks under a password, with the other items of a reused one.
4. **Exclude from Audit** and Include in Audit, in the Item menu and the right-click menu, kept in the vault: **contents
   format 10**. Formats 1 to 9 are still read. Synced and merged; an import leaves it as it is.
5. Not in it: expired passwords, passkeys, websites with one-time codes.

## How it is kept (the coder's, taken in autonomous mode)

1. A password is a field of the kind Password; a PIN or a card's code is not one.
2. The estimate is the cheapest way to build the password from random characters, common words (about 200, English and
   Dutch, written here), what reads as a word, dates, sequences, keyboard rows, repeats, and the item's title, user name
   and website. A password is looked at to its 128th character.
3. At most 4 requests at once; a failed check is tried again with Check now and at the next unlock, the leaks also after
   the next change. An answer without hashes (a hotel's sign-in page) is a failure.
4. A breach counts for its domain and everything under it; unverified, invented and spam-list entries do not count.
5. The score is rounded down, so 100% means every password; green from 90%, amber from 60%, red below.
6. The breaches' card and list stand under the same tick box as the leaks'.
